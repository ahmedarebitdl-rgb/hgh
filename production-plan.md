# Production Plan — "Every Insane Scam That Actually Worked" (8:00, 48 blocks)

**Format:** 48 × 10-second blocks · Seedance 2.0 std · 1080p · 16:9 · mascot character · no subtitles
**Style key job id:** `bfa8ab83-1ad0-4539-b31f-8a8ed163f30f` (attach as `image_references` on every clip)
**STYLE tokens (used in every prompt):** flat 2D hand-drawn cartoon, thick sketchy black ink outlines, characters with oversized round white heads, small black dot eyes, expressive scribbly mouths, stick-thin black limbs, grungy paper-texture backgrounds in muted color washes, comic chaotic energy, non-photorealistic
**Mascot ("the Host"):** round white head, smug half-grin, rumpled brown suit, crooked tie. Historical characters are the same body type with costume variants.
**NEGATIVE (every prompt):** color drift, photorealism, 3D render, live action, lip-sync, captions, on-screen text, letters, watermark
**Clips are silent** (`generate_audio: false`) — narration is laid over each block at assembly; music bed added in YouTube editor afterward.

## Blocks

Each block: narration line (~20–24 words ≈ 8–9s speech) + SCENE/MOTION for the video prompt.

### Cold open + framing

**Block 1**
NARRATION: In nineteen twenty five, a man sold the Eiffel Tower for scrap metal. One month later, he came back and sold it again.
SCENE: The Host mascot in his brown suit stands proudly beside a huge cartoon Eiffel Tower with a hanging wooden SOLD-style blank tag, second identical buyer character arriving with a giant money sack. MOTION: slow push-in, tower wobbles slightly, money sack bounces.

**Block 2**
NARRATION: These are the most ridiculous scams in history that actually worked. And the last one on this list helped start a revolution.
SCENE: The Host gestures at a wall of six framed cartoon portraits of round-headed schemers; the last frame glows ominously with tiny flames around it. MOTION: lateral dolly across the portrait wall, ending on the burning frame.

**Block 3**
NARRATION: Quick disclaimer, I am not a financial advisor. But I did read four whole articles about each of these scams. So, close enough.
SCENE: The Host sits at a cluttered desk with a tiny nameplate and a leaning tower of papers, shrugging with palms up, confident smug face. MOTION: static shot, papers slowly slide off the desk, Host shrug loops.

### Segment 1 — Victor Lustig sells the Eiffel Tower (Blocks 4–9)

**Block 4**
NARRATION: Victor Lustig was a con man who read that Paris found the Eiffel Tower expensive to maintain. He saw a business opportunity.
SCENE: A pencil-mustached round-head man in a dapper coat reads a newspaper at a Paris café, eyes turning into little dollar signs, Eiffel Tower in background. MOTION: slow zoom to his face as the dollar-sign eyes pop.

**Block 5**
NARRATION: He forged government documents, gave himself a fake ministry title, and invited six scrap metal dealers to a very secret meeting.
SCENE: Lustig character stamping papers at a desk piled with fake seals and ribbons, then six identical businessmen round-heads filing into a fancy hotel room. MOTION: quick cut feel, stamp slams rhythmically, dealers shuffle in a line.

**Block 6**
NARRATION: The pitch was simple. The government is quietly selling seven thousand tons of iron, sealed bids only, and please tell absolutely nobody.
SCENE: Lustig at a chalkboard-style easel showing a crude drawing of the Eiffel Tower being cut into pieces, dealers leaning in, one holding a finger to his lips. MOTION: slow arc around the table, easel drawing wiggles.

**Block 7**
NARRATION: Lustig picked the most insecure dealer, a man desperate for status, and even demanded a bribe on top. The dealer paid both.
SCENE: A sweating nervous round-head dealer hands over two bulging money sacks, one big one small, while Lustig accepts with a gracious villain smile. MOTION: push-in on the handshake, sweat drops fly comically.

**Block 8**
NARRATION: The victim was too embarrassed to call the police. So Lustig waited a month, returned to Paris, and sold the tower again.
SCENE: The embarrassed dealer hides under his desk blushing; cut-style split shows Lustig strolling back into Paris with a suitcase, tipping his hat at the Eiffel Tower. MOTION: gentle seesaw between the two halves of the split scene.

**Block 9**
NARRATION: The second buyer did go to the police. Lustig fled to America, where he was arrested years later for a completely different scam.
SCENE: Lustig sprints up a ship gangplank leaving a dust cloud, then a mugshot-style framing of him smiling in front of a height chart with no readable markings. MOTION: fast lateral run then hard settle into the static mugshot pose.

### Segment 2 — The Cardiff Giant (Blocks 10–15)

**Block 10**
NARRATION: In eighteen sixty nine, two workers digging a well in New York uncovered a ten foot tall petrified stone man.
SCENE: Two overalls-wearing round-head workers in a dirt pit stare at a huge grey stone giant lying in the ground, shovels dropped, jaws open. MOTION: crane-style rise revealing the full giant in the pit.

**Block 11**
NARRATION: Scientists said it was obviously a statue. The public said great, here is fifty cents, let us see the giant.
SCENE: A scientist round-head with glasses points dismissively at the stone giant while a massive queue of excited visitors snakes past him holding coins. MOTION: the queue shuffles forward continuously past the protesting scientist.

**Block 12**
NARRATION: The giant was planted by George Hull, an atheist who lost an argument with a preacher and absolutely refused to move on.
SCENE: Two round-heads arguing outside a small church, one in a preacher collar, the other storming off with a giant thundercloud scribble over his head. MOTION: storm-off walk cycle, scribble cloud crackles.

**Block 13**
NARRATION: He spent a fortune carving a gypsum man, aged it with acid and needles, then buried it on his cousin's farm for a year.
SCENE: Hull character in goggles gleefully distressing a huge stone figure with dripping bottles and needles in a barn workshop at night, then patting soil over it. MOTION: candle flicker, quick playful jump between workshop and burial.

**Block 14**
NARRATION: The farm made thousands a week. It got so big that showman P T Barnum offered a fortune to buy the giant.
SCENE: Coins rain over a farmhouse; a flamboyant round-head in a ringmaster coat and top hat arrives holding an enormous money bag. MOTION: coin rain sparkles, ringmaster struts in from frame right.

**Block 15**
NARRATION: When the owners refused, Barnum built his own fake giant and told everyone the original fake was the fake. His copy earned more.
SCENE: Two identical stone giants displayed side by side in rival tents, each with a hand-painted arrow sign pointing at the other, crowds flowing into Barnum's tent. MOTION: slow symmetric pull-back revealing both tents, crowd drifts one way.

### Segment 3 — Charles Ponzi (Blocks 16–22)

**Block 16**
NARRATION: Next, the man so good at scamming they named the entire genre after him. Charles Ponzi, Boston, nineteen twenty.
SCENE: A dapper round-head with slicked hair and a cane poses proudly in front of a Boston office door, tipping his straw hat. MOTION: confident pose hold, subtle sparkle glints on his grin.

**Block 17**
NARRATION: Ponzi found a real loophole. International postal coupons bought cheap in Italy could be redeemed in America for a profit.
SCENE: A cartoon map showing Italy and America, a tiny stamp-like coupon hopping across the Atlantic and growing bigger on arrival. MOTION: the coupon hops in arcs across the ocean, inflating each hop.

**Block 18**
NARRATION: The problem was scale. Doing it for real would require shipping entire warehouses of stamp coupons across the Atlantic Ocean.
SCENE: A tiny rowboat absurdly overloaded with mountains of paper coupons sinking slightly, Ponzi character scratching his head on the dock. MOTION: boat wobbles and sinks lower, coupons flutter off the pile.

**Block 19**
NARRATION: His solution was elegant. Do not do it. Just promise fifty percent returns and pay old investors with new investors' money.
SCENE: Ponzi at a chalkboard easel where the diagram is just a circle of arrows chasing each other around a dollar-sign scribble, nodding investors watching. MOTION: the arrow circle spins continuously, investors nod in sync.

**Block 20**
NARRATION: At his peak Ponzi collected two hundred fifty thousand dollars a day, and people were mortgaging their houses to get in.
SCENE: A queue of round-heads stretches around the block carrying money bags and one literally carrying a small house on his back toward Ponzi's office. MOTION: steady conveyor-like queue shuffle, house bobs.

**Block 21**
NARRATION: A journalist pointed out his scheme required one hundred sixty million coupons, and only about twenty seven thousand existed on Earth.
SCENE: A journalist round-head with a press hat holds up a newspaper with a scribbled chart on it, standing alone looking exasperated. MOTION: he waves the paper, a tumbleweed-style paper ball rolls past.

**Block 22**
NARRATION: The investors responded to this math by giving Ponzi even more money. It collapsed in eight months and took six banks down.
SCENE: The crowd sprints past the journalist trampling him slightly, then six cartoon bank buildings tilt and crumble like dominoes behind Ponzi's office. MOTION: crowd rush left to right, banks topple in sequence.

### Segment 4 — Gregor MacGregor and Poyais (Blocks 23–30)

**Block 23**
NARRATION: In eighteen twenty one, a Scottish soldier named Gregor MacGregor arrived in London claiming to be the prince of Poyais.
SCENE: A round-head in a grand military uniform with epaulettes and a sash bows theatrically before impressed London high-society round-heads under gas lamps. MOTION: deep bow, monocles pop off the impressed crowd.

**Block 24**
NARRATION: Poyais was a paradise in Central America, with fertile soil, a beautiful capital city, and rivers that sparkled with gold.
SCENE: A dreamy storybook vision inside a thought bubble: lush green hills, a gleaming little capital with an opera house, a river glittering with gold chunks. MOTION: gentle pan across the dream landscape, glitter twinkles.

**Block 25**
NARRATION: It had a flag, a constitution, a currency, and a three hundred fifty five page guidebook. It did not have existence.
SCENE: MacGregor proudly displays a table of props: a flag, a thick book, a stack of ornate banknotes; behind him the dream bubble quietly pops. MOTION: bubble pop deflates the background to blank paper texture.

**Block 26**
NARRATION: The land was real. It was empty jungle in Honduras. Everything else, the city, the opera house, the gold, was invented.
SCENE: A wide shot of dense scribbly jungle with absolutely nothing in it; ghost outlines of a city and opera house flicker and vanish. MOTION: ghost outlines fade in and out over the static jungle.

**Block 27**
NARRATION: MacGregor sold land certificates, raised a two hundred thousand pound bond in London, and exchanged settlers' life savings for his homemade money.
SCENE: A busy exchange counter where settlers hand over coin purses and receive crisp ornate play-money bills, MacGregor stamping certificates rapid-fire. MOTION: rhythmic stamping, bills fan out across the counter.

**Block 28**
NARRATION: Two hundred fifty settlers sailed to Poyais. They found no port, no city, no opera. Just jungle. Fewer than fifty survived.
SCENE: Settlers stand on an empty beach holding luggage and useless banknotes, one wearing a full opera outfit, facing a silent wall of jungle. MOTION: very slow push toward the jungle wall, palm fronds sway, mood sombre.

**Block 29**
NARRATION: When the scandal broke, MacGregor fled to France, where he immediately began selling Poyais again, this time to the French.
SCENE: MacGregor sprints across a cartoon map from Britain to France carrying his flag and prop table, sets the same booth up instantly under a French flag. MOTION: dash across the map, booth reassembles with a snap.

**Block 30**
NARRATION: He was arrested, tried, and somehow acquitted. He retired to Venezuela with a military pension and died a respected citizen.
SCENE: MacGregor shrugs cheerfully in a courtroom dock holding his crayon-style map, then relaxes in a hammock between palm trees wearing medals. MOTION: crossfade-style transition from courtroom shrug to gently swinging hammock.

### Segment 5 — The Great Diamond Hoax (Blocks 31–37)

**Block 31**
NARRATION: In eighteen seventy two, two dusty prospectors walked into a San Francisco bank with a bag full of uncut diamonds.
SCENE: Two grizzled round-heads in worn hats and boots plop a lumpy sack onto a marble bank counter, sparkling stones spilling out, teller frozen. MOTION: stones tumble and sparkle, teller's pupils turn into diamond shapes.

**Block 32**
NARRATION: They acted extremely cagey about where the diamonds came from, which turned out to be the greatest sales technique ever discovered.
SCENE: The prospectors clutch the bag defensively while wealthy round-heads in top hats crowd around, practically climbing over each other with money. MOTION: crowd presses in, prospectors lean back further and further.

**Block 33**
NARRATION: Investors begged to be let in. The prospectors reluctantly showed one representative their secret diamond field, blindfolded, in the Wyoming wilderness.
SCENE: A blindfolded businessman round-head is led on a winding path through scribbly mountains by the two prospectors, all on mules. MOTION: bouncy mule ride along a comically looping trail.

**Block 34**
NARRATION: The field was incredible. Kick the dirt, find a diamond. Rubies and sapphires just sitting there in the ground.
SCENE: The businessman kicks soil and gems pop out everywhere like sparkling popcorn; he holds up handfuls, overjoyed, on a mesa field. MOTION: gems pop from the ground in little bursts wherever he steps.

**Block 35**
NARRATION: Because the prospectors had bought cheap gem rejects in London and planted them in the soil like a farmer plants potatoes.
SCENE: Nighttime flashback tone: the two prospectors on hands and knees delicately tucking gems into little soil holes and patting them like seedlings, watering can nearby. MOTION: tender planting loop, moon scribble glows.

**Block 36**
NARRATION: Tiffany's misjudged the value. A mining engineer certified the field. Twenty five of the richest men in San Francisco bought in.
SCENE: A jeweler with a loupe holds a gem beside a wildly tall price arrow; an engineer stamps APPROVED-style blank seal; a row of rich round-heads signs a giant document. MOTION: assembly-line rhythm across the three vignettes.

**Block 37**
NARRATION: Then a government geologist noticed gems only appeared in disturbed soil, and that rubies and diamonds never occur together. Geologically impossible.
SCENE: A geologist round-head holds a ruby in one hand and a diamond in the other, staring deadpan directly at the viewer like a sitcom character. MOTION: static hold on the deadpan stare, one slow blink.

### Segment 6 — The Diamond Necklace Affair (Blocks 38–45)

**Block 38**
NARRATION: And finally, the scam that humiliated a queen and helped light the fuse on the French Revolution. Meet Jeanne de la Motte.
SCENE: A regal round-head woman in a modest but clever dress curtsies before the gilded gates of Versailles, a tiny lit fuse scribble trailing behind her. MOTION: slow push through the gates as the fuse sparkles.

**Block 39**
NARRATION: Jeanne was a broke aristocrat who told a wealthy cardinal she was best friends with Queen Marie Antoinette. She had never met her.
SCENE: Jeanne whispers behind a fan to a plump cardinal round-head in red robes; a thought bubble shows her arm in arm with the queen, drawn extra crudely. MOTION: fan flutters, the crude thought bubble wobbles.

**Block 40**
NARRATION: She forged letters from the queen. When the cardinal wanted a meeting, she hired a lookalike for thirty seconds in a dark garden.
SCENE: A moonlit garden where a woman in an oversized queen wig hands the kneeling cardinal a single rose, Jeanne watching from behind a hedge. MOTION: slow reveal over the hedge, rose handoff is dramatic and brief.

**Block 41**
NARRATION: It worked. So Jeanne told him the queen secretly wanted a diamond necklace worth fifteen million dollars today, purchased discreetly through him.
SCENE: The awestruck cardinal gazes at an absurdly huge multi-tiered diamond necklace displayed on a velvet bust in a jeweler's window. MOTION: necklace sparkles in waves, cardinal's face presses to the glass.

**Block 42**
NARRATION: The jewelers handed the cardinal the necklace. The cardinal handed it to the queen's courier. The courier was Jeanne's husband.
SCENE: A relay handoff across three panels: jewelers to cardinal, cardinal to a courier round-head in livery whose fake mustache is visibly slipping. MOTION: smooth left-to-right relay, mustache slides slowly.

**Block 43**
NARRATION: He took it to London and sold it stone by stone. When the bill arrived, the whole thing exploded publicly.
SCENE: The husband at a market stall labeled with a blank sign, the giant necklace visibly shorter with each customer; then a huge invoice scroll unrolls onto a desk. MOTION: necklace shrinks in steps, invoice unrolls violently.

**Block 44**
NARRATION: The cardinal was arrested in the palace of Versailles and acquitted at trial, which somehow made the queen look even guiltier.
SCENE: Guards escort the cardinal in full ceremonial robes down a mirrored palace hall; in a courtroom he is released while a portrait of the queen frowns. MOTION: escort march, then the portrait's frown deepens.

**Block 45**
NARRATION: Marie Antoinette had done nothing, but France never forgave her. Napoleon later called the necklace affair one cause of the revolution.
SCENE: Jeanne sits atop a small pile of diamonds calmly watching a tiny cartoon Versailles glow orange on the horizon, torch-carrying crowd silhouettes marching past. MOTION: slow zoom out, crowd flows across the foreground.

### Outro (Blocks 46–48)

**Block 46**
NARRATION: So what did we learn. People do not fall for scams because they are stupid. They fall for what they already want to believe.
SCENE: The Host back at his cluttered desk, now wearing a monocle for no reason, counting off on stick fingers while the six schemers' portraits hang behind. MOTION: gentle finger-count gestures, portraits sway.

**Block 47**
NARRATION: Status, treasure, forgiveness, a sure thing. Every scam on this list just handed someone the exact story they wanted to hear.
SCENE: Four floating icons circle the Host's head: a crown, a gem, a rose, a rising chart scribble, each briefly hypnotizing a different round-head victim below. MOTION: icons orbit, victims' eyes turn to spirals one by one.

**Block 48**
NARRATION: Anyway, I am selling early access to next week's video for fifty dollars. Payment accepted in postal reply coupons only. Subscribe.
SCENE: The Host slides a suspicious contract and quill across the desk directly toward the viewer, grinning, one eyebrow raised, a tiny bell icon on the desk. MOTION: contract slides to fill lower frame, Host winks, bell wiggles.
