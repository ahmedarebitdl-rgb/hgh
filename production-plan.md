# Production Plan — "Every Insane Scam That Actually Worked" (5:00, 30 blocks)

**Format:** 30 × 10-second blocks · Seedance 2.0 std · 1080p · 16:9 · mascot character · no subtitles
**Voice:** Arthur (preset, `30fc8796-ceb6-4a66-b3a7-4a145ef7f346`) on all blocks
**Style key job id:** `bfa8ab83-1ad0-4539-b31f-8a8ed163f30f` (attach as `image_references` on every clip)
**STYLE tokens (used in every prompt):** flat 2D hand-drawn cartoon, thick sketchy black ink outlines, characters with oversized round white heads, small black dot eyes, expressive scribbly mouths, stick-thin black limbs, grungy paper-texture backgrounds in muted color washes, comic chaotic energy, non-photorealistic
**Mascot ("the Host"):** round white head, smug half-grin, rumpled brown suit, crooked tie. Historical characters are the same body type with costume variants.
**NEGATIVE (every prompt):** color drift, photorealism, 3D render, live action, lip-sync, captions, on-screen text, letters, watermark
**Clips are silent** (`generate_audio: false`) — narration is laid over each block at assembly; music bed added in the YouTube editor afterward.

**Budget:** 30 clips × 90 = 2,700 + 30 voice takes × 0.8 = ~24 → **~2,725 credits** (+ retry headroom).

## Blocks

### Cold open + framing (1–3)

**Block 1**
NARRATION: In nineteen twenty five, a man sold the Eiffel Tower for scrap metal. One month later, he came back and sold it again.
SCENE: The Host mascot in his brown suit stands proudly beside a huge cartoon Eiffel Tower with a hanging blank wooden sale tag, a second identical buyer arriving with a giant money sack. MOTION: slow push-in, tower wobbles slightly, money sack bounces.

**Block 2**
NARRATION: These are the most ridiculous scams in history that actually worked. And the last one on this list helped start a revolution.
SCENE: The Host gestures at a wall of five framed cartoon portraits of round-headed schemers; the last frame glows ominously with tiny flames around it. MOTION: lateral dolly across the portrait wall, ending on the burning frame.

**Block 3**
NARRATION: Quick disclaimer, I am not a financial advisor. But I did read four whole articles about each of these scams. So, close enough.
SCENE: The Host sits at a cluttered desk with a tiny blank nameplate and a leaning tower of papers, shrugging with palms up, confident smug face. MOTION: static shot, papers slowly slide off the desk, Host shrug loops.

### Segment 1 — Victor Lustig sells the Eiffel Tower (4–8)

**Block 4**
NARRATION: Victor Lustig was a con man who read that Paris found the Eiffel Tower expensive to maintain. He saw a business opportunity.
SCENE: A pencil-mustached round-head man in a dapper coat reads a newspaper at a Paris café, eyes turning into little dollar signs, Eiffel Tower in background. MOTION: slow zoom to his face as the dollar-sign eyes pop.

**Block 5**
NARRATION: He forged documents, gave himself a fake ministry title, and invited six scrap metal dealers to a very secret hotel meeting.
SCENE: Lustig character stamping papers at a desk piled with fake seals and ribbons, then six identical businessmen round-heads filing into a fancy hotel room. MOTION: stamp slams rhythmically, dealers shuffle in a line.

**Block 6**
NARRATION: The pitch. The government is quietly selling seven thousand tons of iron, sealed bids only, and please tell absolutely nobody.
SCENE: Lustig at a chalkboard-style easel showing a crude drawing of the Eiffel Tower cut into pieces, dealers leaning in, one holding a finger to his lips. MOTION: slow arc around the table, easel drawing wiggles.

**Block 7**
NARRATION: Lustig picked the most insecure dealer and even demanded a bribe on top of the price. The dealer paid them both.
SCENE: A sweating nervous round-head dealer hands over two bulging money sacks, one big one small, while Lustig accepts with a gracious villain smile. MOTION: push-in on the handshake, sweat drops fly comically.

**Block 8**
NARRATION: The victim was too embarrassed to call the police. So Lustig waited one month, came back to Paris, and sold the tower again.
SCENE: The embarrassed dealer hides under his desk blushing; split scene shows Lustig strolling back into Paris with a suitcase, tipping his hat at the Eiffel Tower. MOTION: gentle seesaw between the two halves of the split scene.

### Segment 2 — Charles Ponzi (9–13)

**Block 9**
NARRATION: Next, the man so good at scamming they named the entire genre after him. Charles Ponzi, Boston, nineteen twenty.
SCENE: A dapper round-head with slicked hair and a cane poses proudly in front of a Boston office door, tipping his straw hat. MOTION: confident pose hold, subtle sparkle glints on his grin.

**Block 10**
NARRATION: Ponzi found a real loophole. International postal coupons bought cheap in Italy could be redeemed in America for a profit.
SCENE: A cartoon map showing Italy and America, a tiny stamp-like coupon hopping across the Atlantic and growing bigger on arrival. MOTION: the coupon hops in arcs across the ocean, inflating each hop.

**Block 11**
NARRATION: Doing it for real meant shipping warehouses of coupons across the ocean. His solution was elegant. Just do not do it.
SCENE: A tiny rowboat absurdly overloaded with mountains of paper coupons sinking slightly; Ponzi shrugs on the dock and walks away whistling. MOTION: boat wobbles lower, coupons flutter, Ponzi strolls off.

**Block 12**
NARRATION: He promised fifty percent returns and paid old investors with new investors' money. Soon people were mortgaging houses to get in.
SCENE: A queue of round-heads stretches around the block carrying money bags, one literally carrying a small house on his back toward Ponzi's office. MOTION: steady conveyor-like queue shuffle, house bobs.

**Block 13**
NARRATION: A journalist proved the math was impossible. Investors responded by investing harder. It collapsed and took six banks down with it.
SCENE: A journalist round-head waves a newspaper with a scribbled chart while the crowd sprints past trampling him; behind, six cartoon bank buildings topple like dominoes. MOTION: crowd rush left to right, banks fall in sequence.

### Segment 3 — Gregor MacGregor and Poyais (14–18)

**Block 14**
NARRATION: In eighteen twenty one, a Scottish soldier named Gregor MacGregor arrived in London claiming to be the prince of Poyais.
SCENE: A round-head in a grand military uniform with epaulettes and a sash bows theatrically before impressed London high-society round-heads under gas lamps. MOTION: deep bow, monocles pop off the impressed crowd.

**Block 15**
NARRATION: Poyais was a paradise in Central America. Fertile soil, a beautiful capital, rivers sparkling with gold. It did not exist.
SCENE: A dreamy storybook vision inside a thought bubble: lush hills, a gleaming little capital with an opera house, a glittering river; the bubble quietly pops to blank paper. MOTION: gentle pan across the dream, then deflating pop.

**Block 16**
NARRATION: He printed a flag, a constitution, a currency, and a guidebook, then exchanged settlers' life savings for his homemade money.
SCENE: A busy exchange counter where settlers hand over coin purses and receive crisp ornate play-money bills, MacGregor stamping certificates rapid-fire. MOTION: rhythmic stamping, bills fan out across the counter.

**Block 17**
NARRATION: Two hundred fifty settlers sailed to Poyais. They found no port, no city, no opera. Just jungle. Fewer than fifty survived.
SCENE: Settlers stand on an empty beach holding luggage and useless banknotes, one wearing a full opera outfit, facing a silent wall of scribbly jungle. MOTION: very slow push toward the jungle wall, palm fronds sway, mood sombre.

**Block 18**
NARRATION: MacGregor fled to France and immediately started selling Poyais again. He was tried, acquitted, and died a respected citizen.
SCENE: MacGregor dashes across a cartoon map from Britain to France and reassembles his sales booth under a French flag; then relaxes in a hammock wearing medals. MOTION: map dash, booth snaps together, crossfade to swinging hammock.

### Segment 4 — The Great Diamond Hoax (19–23)

**Block 19**
NARRATION: In eighteen seventy two, two dusty prospectors walked into a San Francisco bank with a bag full of uncut diamonds.
SCENE: Two grizzled round-heads in worn hats and boots plop a lumpy sack onto a marble bank counter, sparkling stones spilling out, teller frozen. MOTION: stones tumble and sparkle, teller's pupils turn into diamond shapes.

**Block 20**
NARRATION: They refused to say where the diamonds came from, which turned out to be the greatest sales technique ever discovered.
SCENE: The prospectors clutch the bag defensively while wealthy round-heads in top hats crowd around, practically climbing over each other waving money. MOTION: crowd presses in, prospectors lean back further and further.

**Block 21**
NARRATION: They led one blindfolded investor to a secret field in Wyoming where diamonds and rubies were just sitting in the dirt.
SCENE: A blindfolded businessman is led on a winding mountain trail on a mule, then kicks soil and gems pop out everywhere like sparkling popcorn. MOTION: bouncy mule ride, then gem bursts wherever he steps.

**Block 22**
NARRATION: Because the prospectors had bought cheap gem rejects in London and planted them in the soil like a farmer plants potatoes.
SCENE: Nighttime flashback tone: the two prospectors on hands and knees delicately tucking gems into little soil holes and patting them like seedlings, watering can nearby. MOTION: tender planting loop, moon scribble glows.

**Block 23**
NARRATION: The richest men in San Francisco bought in. Then a geologist noticed rubies and diamonds never occur together. Geologically impossible.
SCENE: A row of rich round-heads signs a giant document; cut to a geologist holding a ruby in one hand and a diamond in the other, staring deadpan at the viewer. MOTION: signing frenzy, then static deadpan hold with one slow blink.

### Segment 5 — The Diamond Necklace Affair (24–28)

**Block 24**
NARRATION: Finally, the scam that humiliated a queen and helped light the fuse on the French Revolution. Meet Jeanne de la Motte.
SCENE: A regal round-head woman in a modest but clever dress curtsies before the gilded gates of Versailles, a tiny lit fuse scribble trailing behind her. MOTION: slow push through the gates as the fuse sparkles.

**Block 25**
NARRATION: Jeanne told a wealthy cardinal she was best friends with Queen Marie Antoinette. She had never met the queen once.
SCENE: Jeanne whispers behind a fan to a plump cardinal round-head in red robes; a thought bubble shows her arm in arm with the queen, drawn extra crudely. MOTION: fan flutters, the crude thought bubble wobbles.

**Block 26**
NARRATION: She forged royal letters, staged a thirty second meeting with a hired lookalike in a dark garden, and the cardinal believed everything.
SCENE: A moonlit garden where a woman in an oversized queen wig hands the kneeling cardinal a single rose, Jeanne watching from behind a hedge. MOTION: slow reveal over the hedge, dramatic brief rose handoff.

**Block 27**
NARRATION: So he secretly bought the queen a diamond necklace worth fifteen million dollars today, and handed it to Jeanne's disguised husband.
SCENE: The awestruck cardinal passes an absurdly huge multi-tiered diamond necklace to a courier round-head in livery whose fake mustache is visibly slipping. MOTION: reverent handoff, mustache slides slowly.

**Block 28**
NARRATION: The necklace was sold off stone by stone in London. France blamed the innocent queen anyway, and never forgave her.
SCENE: The husband at a market stall, the giant necklace visibly shorter with each customer; behind, a portrait of the queen frowns deeper while torch-carrying silhouettes march past. MOTION: necklace shrinks in steps, portrait frown deepens, crowd flows.

### Outro (29–30)

**Block 29**
NARRATION: People do not fall for scams because they are stupid. They fall for the exact story they already wanted to believe.
SCENE: The Host back at his cluttered desk, now wearing a monocle for no reason; four floating icons circle his head: a crown, a gem, a rose, a rising chart scribble. MOTION: icons orbit slowly, Host counts on stick fingers.

**Block 30**
NARRATION: Anyway, I am selling early access to next week's video for fifty dollars. Payment accepted in postal reply coupons only. Subscribe.
SCENE: The Host slides a suspicious contract and quill across the desk directly toward the viewer, grinning, one eyebrow raised, a tiny bell icon on the desk. MOTION: contract slides to fill lower frame, Host winks, bell wiggles.
